## Context

The words written beside the data live in the kernel as `CTX`: `CTX.frame()` (the passages, dated,
with author and kind), `CTX.codes()` (the codes on them, with the words they rest on, which way each
points against the person's usual state, said or inferred, and `day`, the day it describes);
`CTX.grep('pattern')`, `CTX.around('2026-08-14', days=3)` and `CTX.on(activity_id)` return text to
print (rows with `as_frame=True`), `CTX.text('C.417')` one passage whole. READ CTX quotes passages
under ids like [C.417]; the report cites one as [C.417] beside [cell 7], as written or in its English
rendering, marked *rendered*. A code is a model's reading of a passage, not the writer's words; the
words and the numbers can be days apart: match a code to them by its `day`, not its passage's date.
Where the report explains a number by what was written, it says what that rests on - passages
quoted, counts of codes, or your reading.
